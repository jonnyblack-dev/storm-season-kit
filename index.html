Kit list

Everything to stay home for 3 days without power, water or shops, building up to 14 days. Quantities are for 2 adults, so scale for your household. Water comes first, then food, light and power, and medical. Items marked Start here are cheap and give the most value.

How much water to store

30litres

Uses 5 L per person per day for drinking, cooking and a basic wash, and about 0.5 L a day for a small dog in summer. Get Ready Queensland's minimum is 10 L per person for 3 days, so this plans above it. Fill the bathtub too when a warning comes, for flushing.

Grab bags

One bag per person, packed and kept by the door in storm season, so you can leave in five minutes. A waterproof dry-bag backpack works well. Ticks are saved to your account when you're signed in, or in this browser.

Don't leave lithium gear (power banks, jump starters) or medicines in a parked car over summer. The cabin can pass 60°C. Keep the car kit in a tub in the garage and load it when a warning is issued.

Pets

Get Ready Queensland says to keep at least 5 days of food and water for each pet, and to plan where they go if you have to leave. Evacuation centres may not take pets unless they're assistance animals.

      Safety

      Most storm injuries happen after the storm, during clean-up and in floodwater. These are the ones that matter most.

      • If it's flooded, forget it.Never drive, walk, ride or let kids play in floodwater. 15 cm of fast water can knock you over and 30 cm can float a car. Floodwater also carries sewage, chemicals and snakes.
      • Stay 8 metres from fallen power lines.Treat every fallen line as live. Call 000, or Energex on 13 19 62 in South-East Queensland.
      • Gas stoves, heaters and generators go outside.Butane stoves, BBQs and generators make carbon monoxide, which you can't smell. Use them outdoors or with doors and windows wide open, never in a closed room, garage or tent.
      • Lanterns, not candles.Battery or rechargeable LED lights are much safer during a blackout.
      • Store fuel carefully.Keep butane canisters and fuel in a cool, ventilated spot away from the house's ignition sources. Don't store petrol indoors. Keep a fire extinguisher and fire blanket handy.
      • Heat is a hazard too.Power often stays off in hot, humid weather after a storm. Drink water, use battery fans, rest in the coolest room, and check on older neighbours.
      • When in doubt, throw it out.Fridge food that has sat above 5°C for more than about 4 hours should be thrown out. A full freezer stays cold for longer if you keep the door shut. Freeze bottles of water before a storm.
      • Clean up with care.Wear gloves, closed shoes and a P2 mask around mud and mould. Get electrics checked by a licensed electrician before switching back on anything that got wet.
      • Solar doesn't work in a blackout on its own.Grid-connected solar switches off when the grid goes down, unless you have a battery set up for backup.
